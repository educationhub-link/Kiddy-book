🫁 Asthma in Children

Recognise Early • Treat Promptly • Breathe Easier

💙 What is Asthma?

Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disease affecting the lower air passages in the lungs.

Children with asthma have air passages that are hyper-responsive or sensitive to triggers. Asthma may present as recurrent episodes of:
💨 Wheezing
😮‍💨 Breathlessness
🤧 Cough
🫁 Chest tightness
🌪️ What Happens During an Asthma Attack?
Asthma attacks are usually caused by exposure to triggers.

During an asthma attack:
  • Inflammation of the air passages increases.
  • The air passages narrow.
  • More mucus is produced.
  • Airflow becomes obstructed.
  • Breathing becomes more difficult.
Inflammation may also continue even when the child appears well and has few asthma symptoms.
⚡ Common Asthma Triggers
Triggers may differ for each child. Common triggers include:
  • Viral infections, including the common cold and flu.
  • Environmental allergens including house dust mites, animal dander, pollen and indoor mould.
  • Changes in temperature or humidity due to indoor and outdoor climate variation.
  • Environmental irritants including cigarette smoke and smoke haze.
Avoid known triggers where possible.
🚨 Signs of Respiratory Distress
💨 Tachypnoea
Fast breathing
🫁 Chest retractions
👃 Nasal flaring
🗣️ Unable to speak or feed
💙 Cyanosis
Blue lips or fingertips

Early recognition leads to early treatment.
📊 Assessing Severity

⭐ MILD

  • Talking normally
  • Mild wheeze
  • Mild respiratory distress

⭐ MODERATE

  • Increased work of breathing
  • Wheezing
  • Reduced activity

⭐ SEVERE

  • Severe respiratory distress
  • Unable to speak or feed
  • Cyanosis
  • Altered consciousness
🏥 Initial Management

MILD

  • Salbutamol MDI

MODERATE

  • Salbutamol MDI
  • Oxygen to keep SaO₂ ≥95%
  • Oral Prednisolone

SEVERE

  • Oxygen to keep SaO₂ ≥95%
  • IV Hydrocortisone
  • Salbutamol MDI + Ipratropium MDI
  • Escalate care immediately

Always follow the current institutional asthma management guideline.
💊 Medicines Used to Treat Asthma
There are two main groups of asthma medicines:

💨 Relievers

Medicines that work rapidly to open narrowed air passages during an asthma attack.

They provide quick relief of asthma symptoms and help the child breathe better. They are used when needed.

🛡️ Controllers

Medicines that work gradually to control chronic inflammation in the air passages and make them less sensitive to triggers.

They should be used for the prescribed duration and stopped only when advised by the doctor.

Controllers may be prescribed intermittently or continuously depending on the child's response and asthma control.
🫁 Acute Asthma Management Algorithm
⚠️ The supplied inpatient asthma management algorithm is dated April 2015. Verify all treatment steps and medication doses against the current institutional asthma guideline before clinical use.

⭐ Mild

Initial Treatment
  • Salbutamol MDI every 15 minutes × 2.
  • Oral Prednisolone if:
    • Exacerbation >48 hours
    • Past history of severe episode
    • Persistent asthma not responding to increased dose of inhaled steroid
↓
Review: Improving?

If yes: Reduce Salbutamol MDI according to the child's condition.

If no: Continue Salbutamol MDI every 15 minutes × 2 and review again.

⭐ Moderate

Initial Treatment
  • Oxygen to keep SaO₂ ≥95% via nasal prong / mask.
  • Oral Prednisolone.
  • Salbutamol MDI every 15 minutes × 2.
↓
Review: Improving?

If yes: Reduce Salbutamol MDI according to the child's condition.

If no: Continue Salbutamol MDI every 15 minutes × 2 and review again.

If there is still no improvement, transfer to HD.

⭐ Severe

Initial Treatment
  • Oxygen to keep SaO₂ ≥95%.
  • IV Hydrocortisone.
  • IV Aminophylline.
  • Salbutamol MDI + Ipratropium MDI.
  • IV MgSO₄.
↓
Review: Improving?

If yes: Reduce Salbutamol / Ipratropium MDI according to the child's condition.

If no: Transfer to CICU.

💨 How to Use Inhaler With a Spacer
1
Hold the inhaler upright and remove the cap.
2
Shake the inhaler.
3
Attach the inhaler upright into the spacer.
4
Place lips over the mouthpiece and seal tightly.
5
Press the inhaler once firmly and breathe in and out normally for six breaths from the spacer through the mouthpiece.
6
For another dose, wait for 1 minute and repeat Steps 2 to 5.
🧼 How to Care for the Spacer and Inhaler
  1. Clean the spacer at least once a month.
  2. Soak the spacer completely in mild soapy water made by adding 2–3 drops of dishwashing liquid into a container of water for approximately 15–20 minutes. Do not scrub the spacer. Do not rinse it again after removing it from the soapy water.
  3. Allow the spacer to drip dry. Do not use tissue paper or cloth to dry it after washing.
  4. These steps help reduce static charge within the spacer and allow better delivery of medicine from the inhaler to the lungs.
  5. If the spacer needs to be used urgently before it is dry, prime the spacer by actuating 10 puffs of reliever medicine into it after drying.
  6. Wash the plastic holder of the inhaler once a week under warm running water and air dry thoroughly before putting the inhaler back into it.
📌 What Else Should I Take Note Of?
  • Rinse the mouth after using inhaled controller medicine(s).
  • Ensure there is medicine in the inhaler before use.
  • Change to a new inhaler when the dose counter reads “000”.
  • If the inhaler does not have a dose counter, keep track of the remaining doses using a diary or based on the inhaler's starting dose and usage.
  • Do not expose the child to second-hand or third-hand cigarette smoke.
  • Allow the child to rest and avoid exercise during and soon after an asthma attack.
🚨 Consult the Doctor During an Asthma Attack If...
The child:
  • Remains breathless despite reliever medicine according to the asthma action plan.
  • Is unable to feed or take in fluids well.
  • Is unable to speak in complete sentences.
  • Is drowsy, unresponsive or lethargic.
  • Is irritable and difficult to calm.
✅ Ready for Discharge When...
  • No or end-expiratory wheeze.
  • No or mild retractions.
  • Not tachypnoeic on 4-hourly inhaled bronchodilators or 6-hourly oral bronchodilators.
  • SaO₂ ≥95% on room air.
  • Hydration and nutrition adequate.
  • Correct inhalation technique.
  • Parents and patients have been counselled and understand the action plan and adherence to prescribed medication.
