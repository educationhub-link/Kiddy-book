🦠 Gastroenteritis

Recognise • Rehydrate • Recover

💚 What is Gastroenteritis?

Gastroenteritis is an infection of the gastrointestinal tract associated with diarrhoea, usually involving frequent passage of loose or watery stools.

Acute gastroenteritis may present with an increase in stool frequency, typically 3 or more times in 24 hours, with or without vomiting or fever.
The main clinical concern in children with gastroenteritis is dehydration.
🦠 Causes of Gastroenteritis
Gastroenteritis is commonly caused by infection.

Causes may include:
  • Viral: Rotavirus, Norovirus and Astrovirus
  • Bacterial: Salmonella, Campylobacter, Escherichia coli and Shigella
  • Intestinal parasites in selected cases
Viral infection is a common cause of gastroenteritis in children.
🤢 Clinical Presentation
💩 Watery diarrhoea
🤮 Vomiting
🌡️ Fever
😣 Abdominal cramps
💧 Dehydration
⚡ Electrolyte imbalance

Vomiting may occur early in the illness and diarrhoea may continue for several days.
💧 How to Tell if a Child Is Dehydrated
Features that may suggest dehydration include:
🚽 Reduced urine output

Not passing urine for about 6 hours or more
⚖️ Loss of weight
😴 Lethargy
👄 Dry tongue, eyes or lips
💓 Increased heart rate
😢 Reduced or absent tears

🧃 Rehydration Strategy

✅ No Dehydration

  • Encourage increased fluid intake.
  • Oral rehydration solution (ORS) may be used where appropriate.
  • Continue usual age-appropriate diet and feeds.
  • Continue breastfeeding or formula feeding.

💧 Mild / Moderate

  • Enteral rehydration with ORS is preferred where tolerated.
  • The supplied reference describes an ORS fluid challenge of 50 mL/kg over the first 4 hours.
  • Supplement with usual oral fluids if the child refuses sufficient ORS.
  • Monitor response to rehydration.

🚨 Severe Dehydration

  • Treat as an emergency.
  • The supplied reference describes 0.9% sodium chloride 20 mL/kg IV bolus for severe dehydration / shock.
  • If the child remains shocked, reassess promptly and follow the current emergency escalation pathway.
⚠️ Always follow the current institutional paediatric rehydration and IV fluid guideline.
🍽️ Diet & Feeding
👶 Infants
  • Continue breastfeeding and offer feeds more frequently.
  • Formula-fed infants may continue their usual formula if vomiting is not present.
  • Do not feed the baby with water alone.
  • After significant loose or watery stools, oral rehydration fluid may be given according to the child's weight and institutional guidance.
  • If diarrhoea is prolonged, medical review may be required regarding possible secondary lactose intolerance.
🧒 Older Children
  • Continue adequate fluid intake.
  • Usual age-appropriate diet may generally be continued.
  • Soft foods such as porridge, bread, biscuits, soup or mashed potatoes may be offered if preferred.
  • Avoid fatty or fried foods during recovery.
  • Limit excessive simple sugars such as undiluted fruit juice and carbonated drinks.
🤮 If the Child Is Vomiting
  • Offer small amounts of fluid at frequent intervals.
  • Once vomiting has settled, gradually restart normal feeds.
  • For breastfed infants, smaller and more frequent feeds may be better tolerated initially.
  • Older children may restart soft foods when tolerated.
Persistent vomiting or inability to tolerate small amounts of fluid requires medical assessment.
🧪 Investigations
There is usually no need for investigations if the child is clinically well and has normal hydration status.

For moderate or severe dehydration, investigations may include:
  • Renal panel including electrolytes, urea and creatinine
  • Acid-base assessment where indicated
  • Stool culture or stool virology where clinically indicated
  • Septic work-up if indicated
💊 Medications
The main treatment of uncomplicated gastroenteritis is adequate hydration.

  • Most children do not require specific medication.
  • Most bacterial gastroenteritis does not require antibiotics.
  • Antibiotics may be indicated in selected situations such as bacterial dysentery, protozoal disease, very young infants, immunocompromised children or septic patients.
  • Medication for vomiting or other symptoms should be used only when prescribed or advised according to current institutional practice.
  • Antimotility medicines should not be routinely used in children.
🧼 Prevention & Hygiene
Good hygiene helps reduce the spread of gastroenteritis.
  • Wash hands carefully and regularly.
  • Encourage children to wash their hands properly.
  • For newborn babies and infants, ensure good bottle hygiene.
  • Use appropriately cleaned and sterilised feeding equipment.
  • Follow safe food handling practices.
🚨 Seek Medical Attention If...
Consult a doctor promptly if:
  • The child shows signs of dehydration.
  • There is blood in the diarrhoea or vomitus.
  • Vomitus is greenish.
  • There is persistent or severe abdominal pain.
  • Vomiting is persistent and the child cannot tolerate small amounts of fluid.
  • The child appears pale, lethargic or increasingly unwell.
  • There is painful abdominal swelling.
  • Fever persists for more than 5 days.
🚨 Severe dehydration, altered consciousness, shock or significant deterioration requires urgent medical assessment and escalation.
