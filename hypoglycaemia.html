🍬 Hypoglycaemia

Recognise • Treat • Recheck

🩸 What is Hypoglycaemia?

The definition of low blood glucose depends on the clinical context, age, symptoms and underlying condition.

Autonomic effects and hypoglycaemia awareness may occur at a blood glucose level of approximately 3.0 mmol/L.
Paediatric Patients With Diabetes

Hypoglycaemia:
BG / CBG < 4.0 mmol/L
General Reference

Recurrent BG <3 mmol/L, or <3.5 mmol/L when symptomatic, warrants further assessment.
🚨 Signs & Symptoms
Common symptoms may include:
💓 Palpitations
🤲 Tremor
😰 Anxiety
💦 Sweating
🍽️ Hunger
⚡ Paraesthesia
Severe hypoglycaemia may present with:
  • Lethargy
  • Irritability or unusual behaviour
  • Hypothermia
  • Confusion
  • Coma
  • Seizures
In neonates <48 hours, features may include:
  • Apnoea
  • Hypotonia
  • Jitteriness
  • Poor feeding
  • High-pitched cry
🔍 Causes of Hypoglycaemia

🍽️ Poor / Low Substrate

  • Inadequate feeding
  • Gastrointestinal losses
  • Intrauterine growth restriction / prematurity
  • Thin child with poor glycogen stores

🔥 Increased Glucose Utilisation

  • Sepsis
  • Shock
  • Hypothermia
  • Asphyxia
  • Polycythaemia / hyperviscosity
  • Fever

🧬 Hyperinsulinaemic Hypoglycaemia

  • Infant of diabetic mother
  • Excessive maternal intravenous glucose
  • Perinatal asphyxia
  • Congenital causes
  • Islet cell adenoma
  • Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome
  • Dumping syndrome

🧪 Inborn Errors of Metabolism

  • Glycogen storage disorders
  • Defects in gluconeogenesis
  • Carnitine metabolism disorders
  • Fatty acid oxidation disorders
  • Defects in ketone body synthesis / utilisation

💊 Other Causes

  • Drug-induced hypoglycaemia including insulin, sulphonylureas and other implicated medications
  • Accelerated starvation / idiopathic ketotic hypoglycaemia
🩺 History & Clinical Assessment
Important history may include:
  • Symptoms suggestive of hypoglycaemia
  • Birth history including birth weight, gestation and maternal gestational diabetes
  • Neonatal history of hypoglycaemia
  • Previous episodes suggestive of hypoglycaemia
  • Previous gastric surgery or fundoplication
  • Relationship of episodes to meal timing
  • Intercurrent illness with poor feeding
  • Increased glucose requirements such as sepsis or trauma
  • Drug or toxin ingestion
  • Relevant family history
Clinical examination may include assessment for:
  • Macrosomia
  • Dysmorphic features
  • Features of endocrinopathy
  • Skin hyperpigmentation
  • Hepatomegaly or hepatosplenomegaly
  • Hypotonia
  • Blood pressure abnormalities
🍭 Paediatric Diabetes Hypoglycaemia Workflow

< 4 mmol/L

🍬 Serve 15 g of fast-absorbed carbohydrate

120 mL

❌ NO

Repeat 15 g fast-absorbed carbohydrate

Then recheck CBG again after 15 minutes.
✅ YES

Inform the doctor to review the patient and advise the next step.

Consider adjustment of existing insulin or oral hypoglycaemic agent if needed, according to medical advice.
↓
🍽️ If at Meal Time
  • Give insulin injection or OHA as prescribed
  • Do not omit insulin or OHA
  • Serve meal
  • Continue CBG checks as ordered
🍞 If Outside Meal Time
  • Serve 3 crackers OR 1 slice of bread OR 240 mL milk
  • Check CBG 2 hours later
🧪 Investigations & Critical Sample
Where clinically possible, obtain blood and urine samples at the time of hypoglycaemia and before glucose is given.

Treatment must not be delayed if the patient requires urgent correction.

Critical investigations may include:
  • Hormonal studies: insulin, C-peptide, cortisol and growth hormone
  • Glucose and ketones
  • Plasma acylcarnitine profile
  • Free fatty acids
  • Lactate
  • Plasma ammonia
  • Amino acids
  • Urine organic acids
Further investigations may be required depending on the suspected cause.
💉 General Hypoglycaemia Treatment Reference
This section is a general hypoglycaemia reference and is separate from the diabetes-specific CBG <4 mmol/L workflow above.

Follow current institutional orders and escalation pathways.
🙂 If Asymptomatic
  • Aim to restore BG above 3.5 mmol/L through normal nutritional intake where appropriate.
  • Oral glucose or carbohydrate may be administered according to the prescribed institutional protocol.
  • Follow with a carbohydrate serving where indicated.
🚨 If Symptomatic / Unable to Take Orally
  • Administer glucose according to the prescribed emergency treatment protocol.
  • The supplied reference describes 10% IV dextrose 2 mL/kg as a slow bolus over 2–5 minutes.
  • Maintenance IV dextrose may be required and should be adjusted according to the patient's glucose response and medical order.
  • Recheck BG approximately 15–30 minutes after corrective measures, according to the clinical plan.
  • Recurrent or refractory hypoglycaemia requires medical review and escalation.
⚠️ Important Reminders
  • Always assess the child's symptoms and clinical condition, not the glucose value alone.
  • Altered consciousness, seizures or inability to take oral carbohydrate require urgent medical assessment and treatment.
  • Recurrent hypoglycaemia requires further investigation for an underlying cause.
  • Where possible, obtain a critical sample before glucose administration, but never delay urgent treatment.
  • Recheck blood glucose after corrective measures according to the relevant protocol.
🚨 Escalate promptly if hypoglycaemia is severe, recurrent, associated with seizures or altered consciousness, or fails to respond to initial treatment.
