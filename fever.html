🌡️ Fever in Children

Recognise • Manage • Care

❤️ What is Fever?

The human body has a thermostat which regulates body temperature. Normal body temperature is approximately 36.5°C to 37.5°C.

During infection or other illnesses, chemicals released in the body can reset this thermostat, resulting in fever.
Fever is the body's natural way of fighting infection.
📌 A Few Points About Fever
  • Fever is the body's way of fighting infection. However, fever is not caused by infections alone.
  • How high a fever is does not necessarily indicate the severity of the illness causing it.
  • Overwrapping and a generally hot environment can cause a child's temperature to be slightly above normal.
  • Fever caused by viral infections may persist for 5 to 7 days.
  • A small percentage of children aged between 6 months and 6 years may experience seizures with high fever.
  • High fever itself or febrile seizures usually do not lead to brain damage.
🌡️ Temperature Measurement
👶 Below 6 months

Axillary temperature
unless contraindicated
🧒 6 months and above

Tympanic temperature
unless contraindicated
💚 Comfort Measures
💧 Encourage your child to take plenty of fluids.
👕 Dress the child in light, thin clothing.
🪟 Maintain a cool, well-ventilated environment.
❄️ Use a cooling mattress when indicated, if available.
💊 Administer antipyretics as prescribed and monitor their effect.
💊 Fever Medications

Paracetamol

First-line medication
  • Give every 4 hours if temperature is above 38.5°C.
  • Give every 6 hours if temperature is less than 38.5°C.
  • Do not use in infants younger than 3 months old.

Ibuprofen

  • Use when fever remains persistently above 38.5°C, 1–2 hours after paracetamol.
  • Usually given every 6 hours.
  • Do not use in infants younger than 6 months old.
  • In infants aged 6 months to less than 1 year, it may be given every 8 hours.

Diclofenac Na (Voltaren Suppositories)

  • Use when fever remains persistently above 38.5°C, 1–2 hours after paracetamol.
  • Give every 12 hours.
  • Do not use in infants younger than 1 year old unless advised by the doctor.
🌡️ Fever medications usually bring down the temperature by approximately 1–2°C.

Do not be alarmed if the child is still having a fever after medication.

Antibiotics are of no use in viral fevers.
⚠️ Important Medication Precautions
  • Do not give ibuprofen and diclofenac together as they belong to the same class of medications.
  • Avoid either ibuprofen or diclofenac if the child has severe vomiting or a bleeding disorder, due to possible bleeding from the stomach and intestines.
  • Do not give aspirin unless advised by the doctor.
🚨 Seek Medical Attention If...
Consult the doctor if the child:
  • Is feeding poorly.
  • Is vomiting or lethargic.
  • Is very young, especially less than 3 months old.
  • Has difficulty breathing.
  • Is drowsy.
  • Appears sicker.
  • Has decreased urine output.
  • Has new symptoms such as abdominal pain or rash.
