Pain Assessment

Paediatric Pain Scale Quick Reference
Select the appropriate pain assessment tool according to the child's age, developmental ability and clinical setting. Always refer to the latest institutional policy where applicable.

🧸 Which Pain Scale Should I Use?

N-PASS
Neonates in NICU / SCN
NIPS — Neonatal Infant Pain Scale
Age 2 months and below
FLACC Behavioural Pain Assessment Scale
Age 2 months and above
Wong-Baker FACES Pain Rating Scale
Age 3 to 6 years
Numerical Rating Scale (NRS)
Age 6 years and above

👶 NIPS Assessment

Category Score Observation
Facial Expression 0 Relaxed muscles — restful face, neutral expression
1 Grimace — tight facial muscles, furrowed brow, chin or jaw
Cry 0 No cry — quiet, not crying
1 Whimper — mild moaning, intermittent
2 Vigorous cry — loud, rising, shrill or continuous cry
Breathing Pattern 0 Relaxed — usual breathing pattern for the infant
1 Change in breathing — indrawing, irregular or faster than usual; gagging or breath-holding
Arms 0 Relaxed / restrained — no muscular rigidity; occasional random movement
1 Flexed / extended — tense or straight arms; rigid or rapid flexion / extension
Legs 0 Relaxed / restrained — no muscular rigidity; occasional random movement
1 Flexed / extended — tense or straight legs; rigid or rapid flexion / extension
State of Arousal 0 Sleeping / awake — quiet, peaceful, sleeping or alert
1 Fussy — alert, restless or thrashing
For intubated infants: Observe visible mouth and facial movements when assessing crying behaviour, in accordance with local guidance.
