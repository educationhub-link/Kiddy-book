🍼 Expressed Breast Milk (EBM)

Storage • Handling • Breastfeeding Care

💗 EBM Quick Guide

Expressed breast milk should be stored, thawed, warmed and labelled carefully to maintain safe handling and appropriate use.

Use clean containers or milk storage bags and always follow your institution's current policy.

❄️ Thawing Frozen EBM
  1. Frozen EBM should preferably be thawed in the refrigerator.
  2. Transfer frozen EBM into the refrigerator at least 12 hours earlier.
  3. Frozen EBM of about 50 mL usually takes approximately 12 hours to completely thaw in the refrigerator.
  4. Use thawed EBM within 36 hours from the time of transfer from freezer to refrigerator (12 + 24 hours).
  5. For quick thawing, place the bottle of frozen EBM in a warmer at 40°C for 10 minutes.
  6. EBM thawed in a warmer must be used within 1 hour.
  • Do not thaw EBM at room temperature.
  • Do not refreeze EBM that has been thawed.
  • Completely thawed or more than 50% thawed frozen EBM brought into the ward should be stored in the refrigerator and used within 24 hours.
  • Frozen colostrum in syringes usually thaws within 1–2 hours in the refrigerator and should be used within 24 hours from the time of transfer from freezer to refrigerator.
♨️ Warming EBM
  1. Warm EBM from the refrigerator in a warmer at 40°C for 10 minutes.
  2. Use EBM within 1 hour after warming.
  3. Do not microwave or heat EBM directly on the stove.
  4. Small amounts of colostrum in syringes may be fed without warming.

Note: Freshly expressed breast milk can be stored in a cooler bag with ice packs for 24 hours.

🤱 Common Breastfeeding Problems

🌸 Sore Nipples

Sore nipples can be prevented by ensuring proper positioning and latching during breastfeeding.
  • After feeding, express a little milk onto the nipples and allow them to air dry.
  • Going without a bra for short intervals each day may help.
  • If soreness persists, temporarily express breast milk 6 to 8 times a day and feed the expressed milk to the baby.
  • Seek help as soon as possible if the problem persists.

🌼 Engorgement

Engorgement is the accumulation of milk in the breasts, resulting in hardness, overdistension, discomfort or pain.

It may be minimised through demand feeding, including night feeding and early feeding from birth.
  • Massage the nipple, areola and breast to help clear blockage.
  • Apply cold towels or cabbage leaves on the breasts between feeds to reduce swelling.
  • Express breast milk before and after each feed if needed.
  • Analgesia may be used as prescribed or advised to relieve pain.

💧 Blocked Milk Ducts

Blocked ducts occur when milk is not being emptied efficiently. Part of the breast may feel lumpy, hard or sore.
  • Gently massage the nipple, areola and affected area before feeding.
  • Apply cold towels or cabbage leaves on the affected area.
  • Change the feeding position, for example using a football hold.
🚨 Seek medical attention promptly if any part of the breast becomes red and/or painful, or if the mother feels shivery or feverish.

This may indicate a breast infection.

Continue breastfeeding or emptying the affected breast where appropriate to maintain milk supply, and seek advice from the doctor or lactation consultant.
💡 Good Practice Reminders
🧼 Wash hands before expressing or handling EBM.
🍼 Use clean equipment and suitable EBM containers.
🏷️ Label EBM clearly with date, time and volume.
❄️ Store EBM correctly and follow the recommended storage duration.
🕒 Follow the “First In, First Out” principle.
